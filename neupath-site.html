A student-led space for neurodivergent UB students.

NeuPath helps neurodivergent students at the University at Buffalo build career and life skills nobody actually teaches you: networking, interviews, emailing professors, planning, and self-advocacy. All in a lower-pressure, strengths-first way.

Why we exist

Colleges have stepped up on mental health. Career readiness never got the same treatment.

Campus counseling, wellness programming, and mental health outreach have all expanded, and students are using them. What still doesn't exist on most campuses is a standard, structured path for learning how to network, interview, ask for help, or plan a semester. Those are the skills that decide what happens after graduation.

38%

of college students received counseling or therapy in the past year.

61%

of students who screened positive for anxiety or depression received counseling, medication, or both.

1 in 5

college students identify with ADHD, autism, dyslexia, or a related condition.

Sources: Healthy Minds Study, 2024–2025 (84,000+ students across 135 institutions); published research on neurodivergence in higher education.

Support for how students feel has grown. Support for what happens next hasn't, and it leaves a lot of us feeling unprepared and scared.

What we do

Practical, strengths-first, built with neurodivergent students, not just for them.

Every NeuPath event is designed to send you home with something usable: a template, a script, a connection, a plan, or a resource you didn't know existed. We start from what you're good at, not what you're behind on.

  • Résumé and LinkedIn help
  • Networking and interview practice
  • Planning and organization tools
  • Self-advocacy support
  • A community that understands
Programs

What we're building

Networking

Neuro-Friendly Networking Nights

Structured, timed, low-noise networking with question cards and scripts. Participation is optional, so you can observe, write, or sit out a round. You leave with a real contact and a follow-up message ready to send.

Communication

Professor Email & Office Hours Bootcamp

Templates and live editing for the emails that are hardest to send: asking for clarification, following up after a rough exam, requesting accommodations, or asking about research. Plus an office-hours script you can actually use.

Career

Strengths First Story Lab

Turn "I'm awkward" into "I'm direct and thoughtful about how I communicate." Map your strengths, then translate them into résumé bullets, a LinkedIn About section, and interview answers.

Executive function

Executive Function Sprint

A short peer sprint on starting tasks, breaking down assignments, planning a realistic week, and recovering after falling behind. Co-working and body-doubling time included.

Campus

Sensory & Study-Space Map

A student-built guide to campus spaces tagged by noise, lighting, crowding, outlets, and best times to go, so you stop losing months finding somewhere you can actually focus.

Always open

Low-barrier ways in

Drop-in hours, one-time workshops, anonymous question forms, and online participation. You don't have to commit to everything, or to anything, to be part of this.

Questions

Before you join

Do I need a diagnosis to participate?

No. NeuPath is open to neurodivergent students, students who are questioning whether they might be, and allied students. You will never be asked to disclose a diagnosis.

What if I don't want to talk in front of people?

That's expected and planned for. You can participate by writing, using the chat, working in a small group, listening quietly, or passing. You don't have to make eye contact, and you can step out at any time without explaining why.

Is this therapy or a support group?

No. NeuPath is peer support, skill-building, and community. It is not therapy, diagnosis, or crisis counseling. If you need clinical or urgent support, UB Counseling Services and campus resources are the right place, and we can help point you there.

Do I have to come to everything?

No. Most of what we run is designed as a one-time workshop or a drop-in. Come to one thing, come to all of it, or just use the resources.

I'm not neurodivergent. Can I still join?

Yes. Allied students in psychology, education, business, public health, social work, and related fields are welcome, especially if you want to help build programming.
